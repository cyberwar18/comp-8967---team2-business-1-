# Resident interview guide: how people report local problems (KAN-36)

Owner: Mounika Boggarapu · Goal: talk to 3–5 residents of Windsor · About 10 minutes each

## Why we are doing this

Nigel said resident problems (potholes, street lights) go to the city or the ward councillor, not the BIA. Before we design the resident → city report flow, we want to learn how residents report problems today and what would make them do it in an app.

## Before you start

- Say: "I'm a University of Windsor student working on a class project about a neighbourhood map app. This takes about 10 minutes. I won't write down your name or address. Is that okay?"
- Only continue if they agree. Write notes as "Resident 1", "Resident 2", not names.

## Questions

**Warm-up**
1. How long have you lived in Windsor? Which part of the city (general area only)?
2. How do you usually hear about what's happening in your neighbourhood?

**Their last problem**
3. Think of the last time you noticed a problem near your home, like a pothole, a broken street light or garbage not picked up. What was it?
4. Did you report it? If yes, how (311 call, website, app, email, councillor, social media)? If no, why not?
5. Did you find out what happened after you reported it? How did that feel?

**Who is responsible**
6. Do you know who your ward councillor is? Have you ever contacted them?
7. If you had a problem on a main shopping street, who would you contact: the city, the councillor, the businesses, or someone else?

**Using an app**
8. If you could report a problem by tapping a spot on a map, what would you want to add: a photo, a short note, a voice message?
9. After reporting, what would you want to see: who received it, the status (Reported → Acknowledged → Resolved), or a reply?
10. Would you want neighbours to see your report? Should your name be shown?

**Wrap-up**
11. What would stop you from using an app like this?
12. Anything else you'd like to say about reporting problems in your area?
